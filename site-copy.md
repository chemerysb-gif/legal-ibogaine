## What is ibogaine?
A psychoactive alkaloid from a Central African shrub, best known for interrupting patterns — chemical and psychological — that people have spent years trying to out-think.
*Fig. — Tabernanthe iboga, fruiting branch*
Ibogaine comes from the root bark of Tabernanthe iboga, a shrub native to Gabon, used for generations in Bwiti spiritual practice. It entered Western awareness in 1962, when a young New Yorker with a heroin habit took it for the experience — and noticed afterward that his withdrawal never came. That accidental observation has since been documented, in the same direction, by independent clinical teams on three continents.
The liver converts ibogaine into noribogaine, a long-acting metabolite that stays in the body for weeks. The intense, dream-like experience lasts about a day; the pharmacological tail unwinds slowly over the following month.
- Hours 1–24: the acute experience — a waking, dream-like life review
- Weeks 1–4: the window — craving quiet, mood supported, patterns loose
*Fig. — The alkaloid's botanical family*
#### Opioid dependence
The most-studied application: interruption of withdrawal and craving, without substituting another opioid.
#### Alcohol & other substances
Reported benefit across dependencies, with a thinner evidence base than opioids.
#### PTSD, trauma & mood
The 2024 Stanford veterans study documented large improvements in PTSD, depression, and anxiety.
#### Patterns that won't move
A growing group comes not in crisis but stuck: burnout, compulsion, a loop that willpower hasn't broken.
- Not a cure. It interrupts; what happens in the weeks after decides the outcome.
- Not an approved medicine. No large controlled trial completed yet — formal trials are funded and underway.
- Not casual. A serious medical undertaking that belongs in a screened, supervised setting.
If you remember one thing: ibogaine opens a window, not a door marked "cured." The evidence for the window is strong; what you build inside it is the treatment.

## How ibogaine works
Most drugs aim at one target. Ibogaine touches many at once — which may be exactly why it does what it does.
*Fig. — Receptor assay, bench work*
Dependency, trauma responses, and compulsive loops are not character flaws — they are circuits, wired in by thousands of repetitions. Ibogaine appears to act on that wiring directly, in three acts:
#### The reset
Ibogaine interacts with opioid receptors in an atypical way — not substituting like methadone, but appearing to shift the receptor system's adapted state back toward baseline. This is the leading explanation for withdrawal that largely fails to arrive.
#### The review
A long, dream-like state most describe as a life review: memories replayed with unusual detachment — witnessing rather than reliving. Whether this experience is essential or incidental is one of the field's live questions.
#### The long tail
The metabolite noribogaine takes over: a strong serotonin-reuptake inhibitor with opioid-system activity, clearing slowly over weeks. Craving stays quiet; mood is supported; the taper is built in.

## The research
Six decades of observation, one landmark study, and a funded trial pipeline. The state of the science — including what nobody can promise you yet.
*Fig. — Clinical sample processing*
#### Withdrawal interruption
Independent teams on three continents keep documenting the same thing: a single dose can switch off opioid withdrawal — without replacing one dependency with another.
#### Craving that goes quiet
People report the pull going quiet for weeks after treatment — long after the medicine has left the body. The follow-up data backs them.
#### The metabolite explanation
Ibogaine converts to noribogaine, which clears over weeks. Its receptor activity is the leading account of the extended effect window.
#### A neuroplasticity signal
In animal models, ibogaine upregulates GDNF in dopamine circuitry — placing it among compounds that may promote rewiring, not just relief.
#### The evidence ceiling
No adequately powered randomized controlled trial has been completed. Every efficacy claim above rests on observational data.
#### Institutional momentum
A Nature Medicine publication, state trial funding, and engineered analogs have moved ibogaine to a funded research agenda.
The observational signal is too strong to dismiss — and too unconfirmed to declare settled. Both statements are true at once. That is the position of this entire site.

## Safety & protocols
Ibogaine is powerful medicine and must be treated with absolute respect. The risks are real, specific, and — in a properly run clinical setting — systematically managed.
*Fig. — Continuous cardiac monitoring*
The risk, named plainly: ibogaine prolongs the QT interval — the heart's recharge time between beats. In vulnerable people that can trigger dangerous arrhythmias. Documented fatalities cluster around three factors: undetected heart conditions, other drugs still in the system, and settings with no medical screening or monitoring at all.
#### 12-lead ECG
Read by someone qualified to interpret it — before any dose, no exceptions.
#### Blood work
Electrolytes, liver and kidney function — the chemistry that determines how the body handles the dose.
#### Full disclosure
Every medication and substance, verified where possible. Screening only protects you if the inputs are true.
#### A clinician who can say no
History review by a licensed clinician with authority to reject. A screening process that never rejects anyone is theatre.
- Treatment overseen by a licensed physician, supported by nursing staff
- Continuous cardiac monitoring through the session and beyond it — the metabolite keeps working after the visions end
- IV access in place, emergency equipment on site, staff trained in resuscitation
- A realistic plan for reaching a hospital
- Protocols aligned with GITA guidelines, plus newer cardiac-protective measures such as IV magnesium (used in the 2024 Stanford study)
The one-sentence summary: the danger is concentrated where medicine is absent. Depth is only possible because safety comes first — and a provider who treats screening as a formality has already told you everything you need to know.

From the first phone call to the flight home — the honest, stage-by-stage shape of a properly run ibogaine treatment.
*Fig. — The recovery setting*

Medical records, a full medication list, honest disclosure. Some medications need tapering; long-acting opioids like methadone are transitioned to short-acting ones first. A provider who skips this phase is a provider to skip.

ECG, blood work, physician interview — and psychological alignment: what you're carrying in, what you want out. For opioid patients, dosing is timed to the onset of early withdrawal.

## Aftercare & integration
Every follow-up study agrees: the treatment opens a window, and what you build inside it decides the outcome. This page is the second half of ibogaine treatment.
*Fig. — The second half of treatment*
Why this page matters most: across every dataset, long-term outcomes track what happens after treatment more closely than anything about the session itself. People who plan aftercare before they travel do meaningfully better than people who improvise it afterward.
#### Structured support
Therapy or counselling starting within days of returning — ideally arranged before you leave, with someone experienced in addiction or trauma.
#### Processing the experience
The session's material tends to carry personal meaning. Journalling, integration sessions, and facilitated groups all have a place.
#### Environment
Distance from active-use contacts and settings; for some, sober living for the first months. The old environment re-runs the old pattern.
#### Body & community
Sleep, food, movement, and people — unglamorous, and cited constantly by those who sustain the change.
#### Who is my support professional?
Named therapist or counsellor, first session booked for the week you return.
#### Where am I living for 90 days?
And who lives there — is the environment part of the solution or part of the loop?
#### What happens if craving returns?
Precisely: who do you call, what do you do in the first hour.
#### Who knows about reset tolerance?
After ibogaine, opioid tolerance drops sharply — a relapse at the old dose can be fatal. Your household must know this sentence.
#### What fills my weekdays?
Structure for the first month, written down before it's needed.
The one non-negotiable: tolerance reset. It is the single most dangerous fact in post-ibogaine life, and the reason every plan above exists. If someone relapses, the dose that was once routine can now kill. Everyone around the person should know it.

## Ibogaine: What the Evidence Shows in 2026
One entry that compresses the field: what ibogaine is, what has actually been observed in humans, how strong that evidence is, and where the honest uncertainties lie.

### The compound in one paragraph
Ibogaine is an indole alkaloid concentrated in the root bark of Tabernanthe iboga, a Central African shrub with a long ceremonial history in Gabonese Bwiti practice. Chemically isolated in 1901, it drew modern attention for a single anomalous property reported by opioid-dependent users in the 1960s: after one large dose, withdrawal did not arrive. It is metabolized in the liver to noribogaine, a long-lived active compound, giving ibogaine an unusual two-molecule pharmacology — an intense 24-hour experience followed by weeks of residual receptor activity.
### What has been observed in humans
Setting aside anecdote and marketing, the human literature reduces to four consistent observations:
- Acute withdrawal suppression. Structured case series from St. Kitts, Mexico, Brazil, and New Zealand report clinically meaningful reductions in objective opioid-withdrawal scores within hours of dosing — replicated across independent teams that had no contact with one another.
- Extended craving reduction. Follow-up interviews and standardized craving scales show reductions persisting weeks to months, an effect window that outlasts ibogaine itself and roughly tracks noribogaine's slow clearance.
- Variable long-term abstinence. At twelve months, outcomes diverge sharply. A minority sustain abstinence; many relapse partially or fully. Post-treatment structure — therapy, environment, support — is the variable most consistently associated with better trajectories.
- Effects beyond addiction. The 2024 Stanford MISTIC study documented large improvements in PTSD, depression, and disability ratings in 30 veterans with traumatic brain injury — the first ibogaine result published in a top-tier medical journal.

### How strong is this evidence?
Formally: moderate-quality observational evidence, zero completed randomized controlled trials. That distinction matters more here than in most fields, because the population currently reaching ibogaine is exceptional by construction — motivated enough to travel abroad, pay thousands of dollars, and undergo an arduous experience. Selection bias and expectancy effects are not hypothetical concerns; they are the default explanation a rigorous reader must first rule out, and current studies cannot rule them out.
What the observational data can establish is consistency and effect size. When every structured series, in every country, reports the same direction of effect on withdrawal — an outcome that is objectively scoreable — the signal is unlikely to be pure artifact. The 2018 New Zealand series, conducted under a legal prescribing framework with independent follow-up, is probably the single cleanest data point.
### What remains genuinely uncertain
- Effect size under randomization — the gap between observed and true efficacy is unknown.
- Durability — how much of the twelve-month outcome is attributable to the drug versus the aftercare that surrounds it.
- Mechanism — which of ibogaine's many receptor targets actually drives the clinical effect (see the pharmacology entry).
- Generalizability to fentanyl-era opioid physiology, which most published cohorts predate.
### A defensible summary
Ibogaine shows one of the most consistent observational signals in addiction medicine, attached to one of its least complete evidence bases. Dismissal and promotion are both premature; the correct posture is the trial pipeline now being funded.

### Kinetics and metabolism
Orally dosed ibogaine is absorbed and then demethylated by hepatic CYP2D6 into noribogaine. The parent compound clears within roughly a day; the metabolite binds tissue and clears over days to weeks. This is normal for a compound at this stage — but it means mechanistic confidence should lag clinical observation, not lead it.

The emerging synthesis treats ibogaine less as "a psychedelic that treats addiction" and more as a multi-system reset: simultaneous modulation of the opioid system (withdrawal), monoamine systems (mood and craving), and plasticity pathways (relearning), followed by a weeks-long taper courtesy of its own metabolite. Whether that synthesis survives controlled trials is the field's central question — and it is exactly what engineered analogs are designed to dissect, target by target (see Beyond Ibogaine).

## How Ibogaine Interrupts Opioid Withdrawal: Candidate Mechanisms
Suppressing withdrawal without an opioid substitute is ibogaine's signature anomaly. Four candidate mechanisms, the evidence behind each, and why the answer is probably 'several at once'.
*Fig. 04 — The path out, figuratively*
### Contents
- The anomaly, stated precisely
- Candidate 1: opioid receptor resetting
- Candidate 2: the noribogaine bridge
- Candidate 3: glutamatergic interruption
- Candidate 4: monoamine and stress-axis effects
- Why 'all of the above' is the leading answer
### The anomaly, stated precisely
Opioid withdrawal is driven by a well-understood rebound: chronic opioid exposure suppresses noradrenergic and other systems, and removing the drug releases them into overdrive. Every standard remedy either re-supplies an opioid (methadone, buprenorphine) or blunts the rebound symptomatically (clonidine). The ibogaine observation — reported across independent clinical series — is different in kind: withdrawal signs largely fail to appear after a single non-maintenance dose, and the effect persists beyond the parent drug's clearance. Something is modifying the underlying adaptation, not merely masking it.
### Candidate 1: opioid receptor resetting
Ibogaine and noribogaine interact with μ-opioid receptors in an atypical mode — described variously as modulation of receptor signalling or functional antagonism of tolerance mechanisms rather than classic agonism. In animal models, ibogaine reduces morphine tolerance and withdrawal signs without itself substituting. The hypothesis: the compound shifts the receptor system's adapted state back toward baseline, which is exactly what the clinical picture looks like.
### Candidate 2: the noribogaine bridge
Noribogaine's slow clearance means the patient is never abruptly drug-free: a pharmacologically active compound with opioid-system and serotonergic activity tapers itself over weeks. On this account, part of "interruption" is a built-in, self-administering taper — elegant, and consistent with the timing of craving's return as noribogaine levels fall (see the pharmacokinetics entry).

### Candidate 3: glutamatergic interruption
Ibogaine's NMDA antagonism places it in the company of compounds that can interrupt maladaptive learned states. Dependence is, among other things, a deeply consolidated learning process; NMDA-mediated plasticity is central to how such states are maintained and updated. The hypothesis is that the drug opens a window in which the withdrawal-expectation circuitry itself is destabilized and rewritten.
### Candidate 4: monoamine and stress-axis effects
Serotonin reuptake inhibition (mostly via noribogaine) and downstream effects on the stress axis plausibly mute the dysphoria, anxiety, and craving components of withdrawal — the parts that most reliably drive relapse in the first weeks.
### Why "all of the above" is the leading answer
Attempts to reproduce ibogaine's profile with single-target drugs have not succeeded — selective NMDA antagonists, SSRIs, and α3β4 antagonists each capture a fragment at best. Meanwhile, deletion or blockade of individual targets in animals weakens rather than eliminates ibogaine's effects. The parsimonious reading is that withdrawal interruption is an emergent property of simultaneous action across these systems — which is why the analog programs trying to isolate "the" active mechanism (covered here) double as the cleanest mechanistic experiments the field has.

## Noribogaine: Pharmacokinetics of the Long Tail
Ibogaine's most important property may be what it turns into. The kinetics of noribogaine — formation, distribution, clearance — and why a metabolite explains the clinical timeline.
*Fig. 05 — Botanical source material*

### Formation: one demethylation
In the liver, cytochrome P450 2D6 removes a single methyl group from ibogaine, yielding 12-hydroxyibogamine — noribogaine. This is not a minor breakdown product: it is pharmacologically active, in some assays more potent than the parent, and it dominates total drug exposure within hours of an oral dose.
### The kinetic picture
The human numbers come chiefly from the New Zealand ascending-dose studies in healthy volunteers (Glue et al.) plus clinical sampling in treatment cohorts:
- Parent compound: ibogaine plasma levels peak within a few hours and decline to low levels within roughly 24 hours.
- Metabolite: noribogaine appears rapidly, plateaus, and then clears slowly — remaining quantifiable for days and, at the tissue level, likely for weeks. It distributes extensively and shows high brain uptake in preclinical work.
- Exposure ratio: integrated over time, noribogaine exposure substantially exceeds ibogaine exposure after a standard oral dose.
In plain terms: for most of the episode a patient is "on" noribogaine, not ibogaine.

### Why this study mattered
Before 2024, ibogaine research lived in specialty journals and offshore clinics. The MISTIC study (Magnesium–Ibogaine: the Stanford Traumatic Injury to the CNS protocol) changed the field's institutional position in a single publication: a Stanford-led team, standardized psychiatric instruments, and Nature Medicine — one of the most selective venues in clinical science. Whatever one concludes about the data, ibogaine stopped being an un-publishable subject.
### The design, precisely
- Population: 30 U.S. Special Operations veterans with histories of repeated traumatic brain injury and predominantly severe psychiatric symptom burden — PTSD, depression, anxiety.
- Intervention: oral ibogaine at a clinic in Mexico (where treatment is lawful), with intravenous magnesium co-administration intended to mitigate cardiac effects, under continuous monitoring.
- Measures: WHODAS disability ratings and standard clinician-administered scales for PTSD, depression, and anxiety, assessed before treatment and one month after; cognitive testing alongside.
- Design class: open-label, single-arm, pre/post. No placebo, no randomization, no blinding.
### The results
The reported effects were unusually large. Mean disability moved from mild-to-moderate impairment into the normal range at one month. Clinician-rated PTSD, depression, and anxiety scores fell on the order of 80–90% on average, and cognitive measures improved modestly. On the safety side, no serious cardiac events were reported, and the characteristic drug-induced ECG changes were attenuated in the magnesium-supported protocol — a finding of real interest for future trial design.

## A Short History of Ibogaine Science, 1901–2026
From a French pharmacology lab to a Nature Medicine paper: the century-long, repeatedly interrupted scientific career of one alkaloid, in five periods.

### 1901–1960: isolation and obscurity
Ibogaine was isolated from iboga root bark around 1901 by French and Belgian chemists, decades after colonial botanists first described the plant — and centuries into its ceremonial use in the Bwiti tradition of Gabon, a context that long predates and outlasts every scientific period in this entry. Mid-century France briefly marketed a low-dose iboga extract as the tonic Lambarène; pharmacology papers appeared sporadically. Nothing suggested the compound's later significance.
### 1962–1970: the Lotsof observation
In 1962, Howard Lotsof, a 19-year-old heroin user in New York, took ibogaine for its psychoactive effects and noticed that his withdrawal never arrived and his craving was gone. He administered it informally to several dependent friends and observed the same. A single-digit, uncontrolled, retrospectively reported case series — and, unusually for such a thing, one whose central observation has been directionally reproduced by every structured study since. Lotsof spent the following decades as the compound's chief advocate, later patenting its use in dependence disorders.
### 1970–1993: prohibition and hibernation
The U.S. placed ibogaine in Schedule I in 1970 amid the general scheduling of psychedelics, and formal research effectively stopped for two decades. What survived was informal: self-treatment networks and activist "sessions" in the U.S. and the Netherlands, which generated the first systematic protocol knowledge — and, eventually, the case documentation that would define the compound's risk profile for clinical researchers to address.

### 1993–2018: the observational era
NIDA funded early protocol development in 1993, then dropped the program in 1995 — a decision that pushed ibogaine research offshore for a generation. ## Beyond Ibogaine: Tabernanthalog and Engineered Analogs
Chemists are dismantling ibogaine into its useful parts: analogs that keep the anti-addictive pharmacology while shedding the 24-hour experience. The science, the candidates, and the question they secretly answer.

### Why engineer analogs at all
Ibogaine presents drug developers with a bundled deal: promising anti-addictive pharmacology tied to a demanding 24-hour experience, cardiac ion-channel activity, complex metabolism, and a hard-to-patent natural structure. Analog programs try to unbundle it — retain the receptor actions thought to matter, remove the liabilities, and produce something a regulator and a manufacturer can work with.
### Tabernanthalog
The best-known result is tabernanthalog (TBG), reported by David Olson's group in Nature in 2021: a structurally simplified, water-soluble iboga analog. In rodent models it reduced alcohol and heroin self-administration and showed antidepressant-like effects, promoted structural neural plasticity — and did not produce the head-twitch response used as a rodent proxy for hallucinogenic effect, nor the parent compound's ion-channel liabilities in the assays reported. Rodent results are rodent results; but as proof that the iboga scaffold can be decoupled from its heaviest baggage, TBG is the field's landmark.

### The wider candidate set
- Noribogaine and derivatives — the natural metabolite as a gentler, long-acting candidate; human phase-1 kinetics already exist (see the pharmacokinetics entry).

### The supply chemistry
A quieter branch of the same research solves a different problem: wild iboga is slow-growing, and demand pressures the plant in its native Gabon. Semi-synthesis from voacangine (an alkaloid of the more abundant Voacanga africana) and recent total-synthesis routes make ibogaine and its analogs producible without wild harvesting — relevant to sustainability, to Gabonese heritage concerns, and to any future in which a regulator requires pharmaceutical-grade, traceable supply.

Every non-hallucinogenic analog is also an experiment on the field's deepest open question: does the visionary experience contribute to the therapeutic outcome, or is it separable pharmacological baggage? If TBG-class compounds reproduce ibogaine's clinical effects in humans, the experience was incidental; if they fall short, the experience — or something entangled with it — was doing work. Either result would be among the most informative findings in psychedelic science, which is why these programs matter well beyond ibogaine itself. ### Q3: Does it generalize to fentanyl?
Most published cohorts predate fentanyl's dominance. Long-acting, high-potency synthetic opioids change withdrawal timelines and dosing logistics. A passing grade: trial populations that reflect the actual 2026 opioid crisis, with protocols explicitly designed and reported for fentanyl-dependent participants rather than extrapolated from the heroin era.
### Q4: Is the experience necessary?
The analog programs (see Beyond Ibogaine) make this testable: compare ibogaine against a non-hallucinogenic analog, or run dose-ranging designs that decouple pharmacological exposure from experiential intensity. A passing grade is any controlled design that lets the data, rather than ideology in either direction, say whether the visionary component carries therapeutic weight.

Current real-world practice varies enormously — dosing, screening, monitoring, aftercare. Trials will force standardization (the MISTIC magnesium protocol is an early example of exportable protocol engineering), and the standards that emerge will matter far beyond the trials themselves, because they will define what responsible practice looks like wherever ibogaine is administered.

## Ibogaine vs. Suboxone and Methadone: An Honest Comparison
Maintenance medications have decades of evidence; ibogaine has a striking signal and no completed controlled trial. How the two strategies actually differ, who each fits, and why the honest answer isn't a rivalry.
*Fig. 10 — The approved toolkit*

### Two strategies, not two drugs
The comparison people usually want — "which works better?" — starts from a category error. Maintenance medication and ibogaine are not competing versions of the same idea; they are opposite strategies. Buprenorphine and methadone manage dependence: a stable, long-acting opioid replaces a chaotic one, indefinitely if needed. Ibogaine attempts to interrupt dependence: one supervised episode intended to switch the adaptation off and open a drug-free window.
Managing and interrupting can both be right — for different people, at different points in the same life.
### The evidence gap, stated fairly
Here the asymmetry is stark, and pretending otherwise would betray this site's premise. Maintenance treatment is among the best-evidenced interventions in psychiatry: large randomized trials and cohort studies consistently show roughly halved mortality for people in treatment versus out of it. Ibogaine's support is observational — consistent and striking, but without a completed randomized trial. On evidence quality alone, maintenance wins decisively. The honest question is therefore not "which is proven" but "what does each offer the person for whom the proven option keeps failing?"

### Who each approach fits
Maintenance fits people who need stability first: mortality protection, employment, custody, health — the things a daily prescribed dose reliably protects. It is also simply the correct answer for anyone screened out of ibogaine on cardiac grounds (see what still works).
Ibogaine tends to attract a different profile: people for whom maintenance has repeatedly failed or feels like a ceiling; people willing to travel, pay, and shoulder real risk for a chance at a different trajectory. That is a legitimate position — and it deserves the same honesty about the evidence gap that this entry has tried to give.
### Sequencing, not rivalry
In practice the two interact. Long-acting maintenance opioids complicate ibogaine protocols — responsible clinics transition patients to short-acting opioids first, over weeks. And after ibogaine, some people use naltrexone or structured support through the vulnerable window. These are physician-level sequencing decisions, not website-level ones — but knowing the shape of them protects you from any provider who waves the complexity away. ## Frequently asked questions
Straight answers, grouped by topic — from “what is it” to “what does it cost” to “who should never take it.”
*Fig. — The source plant family*
### Basics
[button] What is ibogaine, in one sentence?
A psychoactive alkaloid from the root bark of the African iboga shrub, best known for its reported ability to interrupt opioid withdrawal and quiet craving for weeks after a single dose.
[button] Is ibogaine the same as iboga?
Not quite. Iboga is the plant; ibogaine is one purified alkaloid extracted from it. Clinical settings use measured doses of pharmaceutical-grade ibogaine, which allows precise dosing — something raw bark cannot offer.
[button] Is ibogaine an approved medicine?
No. It is not approved by the FDA, EMA, or any comparable regulator. It is an investigational compound: studied in formal research, prescribable in a small number of countries, and unapproved everywhere else.
[button] Is it a psychedelic like psilocybin or LSD?
It is psychoactive, but different in kind: it acts on a much broader set of brain targets, produces a long dream-like life review rather than classic psychedelic effects, and leaves behind a metabolite that keeps working for weeks. You remain aware and oriented throughout.
[button] Does ibogaine cure addiction?
No. The accurate word is interrupt: it can switch off withdrawal and open a window of reduced craving. What happens in that window — aftercare, environment, support — decides the long-term outcome. Anyone promising a cure is overselling.
### Treatment & eligibility
[button] What conditions is it used for?
Most-studied: opioid dependence. Also used for alcohol and stimulant dependence, and — with growing research attention — PTSD, trauma, and treatment-resistant depression. Many people come simply because a pattern in their life won't move.
[button] Who is ruled out?
People with heart conditions or rhythm abnormalities, certain daily medications, severe liver or kidney impairment, a history of psychosis, and pregnancy. These exclusions exist because ibogaine affects the heart's electrical rhythm — screening is the whole game. See the Eligibility page.
[button] I'm on methadone or Suboxone. Is that a problem?
It complicates timing but doesn't automatically rule you out. Long-acting opioids interact badly with ibogaine protocols, so responsible clinics transition you to short-acting opioids over a period first. Be wary of anyone who says 'come as you are.'
[button] How long does treatment take?
The acute experience lasts roughly 24 hours, but a responsible program runs about a week: screening and preparation, the session, and several supervised recovery days before travel.
[button] Does it work for fentanyl users?
Fentanyl complicates both the pharmacology and the timing, and most published research predates the fentanyl era. Experienced providers have adapted protocols, but this is exactly the kind of case where provider quality matters most — ask directly about their fentanyl protocol.
### Safety
[button] How dangerous is it, honestly?
Ibogaine affects heart rhythm and has caused deaths — almost always where screening was absent, heart conditions went undetected, or other drugs were still in the system. With rigorous screening and continuous monitoring the risk drops substantially, but it never reaches zero.
[button] What does proper screening involve?
A 12-lead ECG, blood work covering electrolytes and liver and kidney function, full medication and substance disclosure, and a medical history review by a licensed clinician with the authority to say no.
[button] What should be in place during the session?
A licensed physician, continuous cardiac monitoring that extends beyond the acute experience, IV access, emergency equipment, staff trained in resuscitation, and a realistic hospital plan. Protocols should align with GITA guidelines.
[button] Why does monitoring continue after the visions end?
Because the active metabolite, noribogaine, remains in the body for days to weeks and continues to affect heart rhythm. The medical episode is longer than the experiential one.
### Practical
[button] Where is ibogaine legal?
It varies completely by country: prohibited in the US and much of Europe, a prescription medicine in New Zealand, and unregulated in countries like Mexico and Costa Rica, where most treatment actually happens. See the Legal Status page for the full picture.
[button] How much does it cost?
Clinic-based programs typically run $5,000–$15,000+ USD for a multi-day stay including medical care. Very cheap offers usually mean the safety infrastructure has been cut — see the Cost page for what drives the price.
[button] How do I choose a provider?
Ask precise questions and listen for precise answers: who screens, what monitoring runs during dosing, how they handle methadone and fentanyl, what aftercare actually includes, and who they refuse. Our Choosing a Provider guide gives you the full checklist.
[button] Will insurance cover it?
Almost never — ibogaine is unapproved, so treatment is essentially always self-funded. Budget for travel and aftercare as well as the program fee.
