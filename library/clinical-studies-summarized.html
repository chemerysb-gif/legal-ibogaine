What counts as a major study

This entry covers human studies with a defined protocol, standardized outcome measures, and peer-reviewed publication. Case reports, surveys without structured instruments, and animal work are excluded — not because they are worthless, but because they answer different questions.

Study-by-study notes

Noller 2018 is the cleanest addiction dataset: legal prescribing framework, independent researchers, standardized instruments (SOWS, ASI), and twelve months of follow-up. Its weakness is size — fourteen participants support direction, not magnitude.

Brown & Alper 2018 replicated the direction of effect in a different country and clinic system, with the familiar observational caveats and meaningful attrition at later follow-ups — and attrition is rarely random.

Mash 2018 contributes scale. Nearly two hundred patients with consistent intake and discharge measures make chance an implausible explanation for the acute effect. It reports discharge outcomes, not long-term ones.

Davis 2017 is a self-selected survey and should be weighted accordingly; its value is convergence with the prospective series on the withdrawal question.

Glue 2015 answers no efficacy question at all — but it is the reason human dosing of noribogaine is characterized, and later trial designs lean on it.

Cherian 2024 (MISTIC) moved the field's center of gravity: a different indication (TBI-related psychiatric symptoms), rigorous instrumentation, and publication in Nature Medicine. It is analyzed line-by-line in the MISTIC close read.

The pattern across all of them

Three regularities. The acute effect on withdrawal appears in every dataset that measures it. The long-term effect is real but heterogeneous and entangled with aftercare. And no study — none — includes a randomized control arm, which caps the strength of every conclusion above.

The study that hasn't happened yet

An adequately powered RCT for opioid use disorder, in a fentanyl-era population, with twelve-month follow-up and pre-registered outcomes. The funding now exists (see Open Questions); the field's credibility over the next five years rests on whether that study is run and what it shows.
