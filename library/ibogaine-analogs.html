Tabernanthalog and other ibogaine analogs: can they work without the long experience?

Chemists are building relatives of ibogaine that aim to keep its effect on drug use while dropping the long visionary state and the strain on the heart. Most of the evidence so far comes from animals, with early human safety data for noribogaine and 18-MC.

Ibogaine analogs are lab-made relatives of ibogaine. Chemists design them to keep its effect on drug use while dropping its long visionary state and its strain on the heart [1][2]. In rodent studies, tabernanthalog and 18-MC cut drug taking without ibogaine's main side effects [1][2]. The analogs are at the animal and early human safety stage [3].

What are ibogaine analogs?

Ibogaine analogs are compounds built on ibogaine's chemical frame and changed on purpose [4]. The relative with the most human data is noribogaine, the compound the body makes from ibogaine [5][6][7].

Three lines of engineered analogs have been tested in animals for effects on drug use. 18-MC came first. In rat studies from the 1990s, it reduced the use of several drugs [2][8].

Tabernanthalog, or TBG, followed in 2021, with results in mice and rats [1]. Oxa-iboga compounds appeared in 2024, tested in rats and in human heart cells grown in a dish [9].

18-MC has reached people. A safety trial in healthy volunteers finished in 2021, at a site in Perth, Australia [3]. It tracked side effects and how the body handles the drug [3].

People following ibogaine research often want to know whether a gentler version is close. Each candidate sits at a different stage. For the wider evidence on ibogaine itself, see Ibogaine: What the Evidence Shows in 2026.

Why do chemists redesign ibogaine?

Chemists redesign ibogaine because of what it does to the heart and how long it lasts [1]. In a Dutch hospital study of people with opioid dependence, the heart's QT interval passed 500 milliseconds in half the patients after one dose [10]. That interval is the heart's electrical reset between beats. In more than four in ten, it was still long a day later [10]. Every patient also lost their balance for a while and could not walk unaided [10].

In the same patients, noribogaine levels stayed far lower than in trials that gave it directly [11]. The QT rise and the unsteadiness tracked ibogaine levels instead [11]. By the team's estimate, a dose more than ten times smaller would be needed to cut the QT risk in a meaningful way [11].

The visionary state is long too. A St Kitts treatment team reported that people who process ibogaine slowly had a very strong experience lasting up to 24 hours [5]. Two people who cleared it very fast had ibogaine levels too low to measure. They reported no dream-like state at all [5].

Lab work shows where part of the heart risk comes from. In cell tests, ibogaine blocks hERG, a potassium channel the heart needs to reset each beat [12].

The design method works like testing a recipe. A cook drops one ingredient at a time to learn which ones carry the flavour. The TBG team built simpler versions of ibogaine, each missing part of its ring structure [1]. They then tested which versions still made rat brain cells grow new branches [1].

One piece of the molecule carried that effect. It was ibogaine's indole ring fused to a seven-membered ring [1]. Keeping that piece and dropping the rest gave compounds that chemists can make in a single step [1].

Do ibogaine analogs reduce drug use without the long experience?

In people, the closest relative with trial data is noribogaine, and it lengthens the heart's QT interval [6][13]. In rodent studies, 18-MC and TBG both reduced drug use without ibogaine's main side effects [1][2].

Given alone to people coming off methadone, noribogaine lengthened the QT interval more at each higher dose [6]. At the top dose, the largest average rise was about 42 milliseconds [6].

In a 2024 trial, healthy volunteers took it in repeated doses [13]. The trial recorded no serious adverse events [13]. Reported side effects included nausea, headache and feeling faint [13]. QT prolongation appeared as a side effect, more often at higher doses [13]. It showed up in most people on the top dose and in none on placebo [13].

In rats, 18-MC reduced the self-administration of morphine, cocaine, alcohol and nicotine [2]. Rat studies found it free of ibogaine's tremor, cerebellar damage and heart slowing [2].

In mice, TBG did not cause the head twitch that researchers use as a stand-in for hallucinations [1]. In cell tests, it blocked the hERG channel about a hundred times more weakly than ibogaine [1]. In mice that binge on alcohol, one dose cut drinking and left their taste for sugar water intact [1].

In rats, oxa-iboga compounds reduced morphine, heroin and fentanyl intake [9]. In human heart cells grown in the lab, they did not show ibogaine's rhythm-disturbing effects [9].

Do the analogs work the same way ibogaine does?

Lab and animal studies show each analog keeping a different part of ibogaine's action. Ibogaine binds many brain targets at once [16]. Picture it as a master key that turns many locks. Each analog is cut to fit one or two of them.

In lab studies, 18-MC's strongest action is blocking one nicotinic receptor, called alpha3beta4 [17].

In cell and mouse studies, TBG works through a serotonin receptor, 5-HT2A, the one classic psychedelics use [1]. In living mice, it added new dendritic spines, the small contact points between neurons [1].

In lab tests, the oxa-iboga compounds act on the kappa opioid receptor [9]. The full map of candidate targets is in How Ibogaine Interrupts Opioid Withdrawal: Candidate Mechanisms.

Noribogaine has been tested for this kind of action in people. In healthy men given small single doses, it showed no sign of acting like morphine or heroin [7]. Pupil size and pain tolerance, two standard tests for that kind of opioid action, stayed unchanged [7].

How long do the analogs last, and how are they made?

Noribogaine, the relative measured in people, stays in the body for days. Its half-life ran 24 to 30 hours in people coming off methadone [6]. In healthy volunteers given small single doses, it ran 28 to 49 hours [7]. Blood levels in that trial peaked 2 to 3 hours after the dose [7].

With repeated doses, it builds up. In the 2024 volunteer trial, peak blood levels on day 8 ran about two to three times those on day 1 [13]. The kinetics are covered in Noribogaine: Pharmacokinetics of the Long Tail.

18-MC depends on a different liver enzyme. In human liver tissue tested in the lab, CYP2C19 did most of the work of breaking it down [18]. The activity of that enzyme varies with a person's genes [18]. Ibogaine relies on another enzyme, CYP2D6 [5]. In St Kitts, most ibogaine had left the blood within a day, while noribogaine levels stayed high at 24 hours [5].

In rats, a single dose of TBG reduced cue-triggered heroin relapse for up to two weeks [1].

Supply is part of the design. TBG dissolves readily in salt water, unlike ibogaine [1].

Ibogaine makes up about 0.3% of iboga root bark [4]. Most ibogaine is made from voacangine, a related compound from the bark of the Voacanga africana tree [4][19].

Harvesting the plant has a cost. Overharvesting of iboga harms the cultures that use it in spiritual practice [19]. Among them is the Bwiti tradition of Gabon and Cameroon, where iboga is central [20].

A new route avoids the plant altogether. In 2025, chemists reported a seven-step synthesis of ibogaine from pyridine, a cheap and widely available chemical [19]. It works at gram scale [19]. The team notes that such a route could ease concerns about ecological sustainability [19]. The same route produced new analogs for testing [19]. More on ibogaine's own targets and kinetics is in The Pharmacology of Ibogaine: Targets, Kinetics, Metabolism.

What is the ibogaine experience that analogs try to remove?

Ibogaine treatment usually moves through two stages. A vivid, dream-like stage comes first, then a calm, thoughtful one [5][21]. In St Kitts, the visions began about 30 to 45 minutes after the dose [5]. The dream-like stage lasted 4 to 8 hours [5].

In that first stage, people see a stream of images. Many of them are memories of past events [21].

About four in ten people in St Kitts saw themselves as a child [5]. More than half felt linked to a higher power or to something larger than themselves [5].

The second stage is quieter. Attention turns to making sense of what came up [5][21]. This stage lasts roughly 8 to 20 hours [21]. Sleep is often hard for up to three days afterwards [21].

In a Dutch hospital study, patients described visions with their eyes closed and vivid memories [10].

The body feels it too. Nausea, vomiting and unsteady walking were the most common side effects in St Kitts [5]. About one person in twenty had a drop in blood pressure on standing up [5].

Noribogaine has also been given to patients on its own. In its first patient trial, the most common effects were changes in how light looked, headache and nausea [6]. Those light changes came about an hour after the dose [6]. In the 2024 repeated-dose trial, one person on the top dose had a spell of confusion [13].

Looking back, most people in St Kitts said they felt a benefit [5]. Nine in ten said it helped with their drug problem [5]. About one in six said they would go through it again [5]. A similar nine in ten said it gave them insight [5].

Researchers developing TBG note that many people credit psychedelic benefits to insights gained during the experience [22].

Where the research goes next

Four directions are open. The first is human data on an engineered analog. The 18-MC volunteer trial has finished [3]. Its safety and blood-level results are the next step.

The second is whether the visions matter. The TBG team suggests that some benefits might come without hallucinations [22]. A trial of a non-hallucinogenic analog in people with addiction would test that idea head on.

Third is heart checks built into every trial. Noribogaine, a close relative, still lengthened the QT interval in people [6][13].

The Dutch ibogaine study offers a model. It monitored each patient for at least a day. It gave magnesium once the QT interval passed 500 milliseconds [10].

The fourth question is which compound does the work. Given on its own to people coming off methadone, noribogaine gave a trend toward lower withdrawal scores that was not statistically significant [6].

For TBG, spine growth is linked to mood-related behaviour in mice [23]. Does the same growth drive its effect on drug seeking? That is still open [22]. The trials that could settle it are set out in Open Questions: What Rigorous Ibogaine Trials Must Answer.

Frequently asked questions

Has tabernanthalog been tested in humans?

Tabernanthalog's published results come from mice, rats, zebrafish and cells grown in the lab [1][15][23]. In those studies, it reduced alcohol drinking and heroin relapse [1].

What are the effects of tabernanthalog?

In lab and rodent studies, TBG acts on the 5-HT2A serotonin receptor [1]. It helps neurons grow new branches and spines [1]. In mice, it had an antidepressant-like effect a day after one dose [1].

How is noribogaine different from ibogaine?

Noribogaine is the compound the body makes from ibogaine, and it lasts much longer [5][6]. Ibogaine's half-life is about 1.6 to 6 hours [5]. Noribogaine's ran 24 to 30 hours in people coming off methadone [6]. Taken alone, it most often changed how light looked and caused headache and nausea [6]. It also lengthened the QT interval [6].

Is there a synthetic ibogaine?

Yes. In 2025, chemists reported a seven-step synthesis of ibogaine from pyridine, at gram scale [19]. Most ibogaine today is made from voacangine, a related compound taken from Voacanga africana bark [4][19].

Has noribogaine been tested in clinical trials?

Yes. Noribogaine has been given to people in three trials with reported results [6][7][13]. Healthy volunteers took small single doses in one and repeated daily doses in another [7][13]. In the third, people coming off methadone took single doses [6]. Their QT interval lengthened more at higher doses [6].

Safety. Ibogaine prolongs the heart's QT interval and can trigger dangerous arrhythmias. Treatment requires an EKG and bloodwork before dosing, continuous cardiac monitoring during, and medically trained staff present. Ibogaine is not safe to take unsupervised.
