Noribogaine: Pharmacokinetics of the Long Tail

Ibogaine's most important property may be what it turns into. The kinetics of noribogaine — formation, distribution, clearance — and why a metabolite explains the clinical timeline.

Contents

  2. The kinetic picture
  3. Mapping kinetics onto the clinical timeline
  4. CYP2D6 and between-person variability

Formation: one demethylation

In the liver, cytochrome P450 2D6 removes a single methyl group from ibogaine, yielding 12-hydroxyibogamine — noribogaine. This is not a minor breakdown product: it is pharmacologically active, in some assays more potent than the parent, and it dominates total drug exposure within hours of an oral dose.

The kinetic picture

The human numbers come chiefly from the New Zealand ascending-dose studies in healthy volunteers (Glue et al.) plus clinical sampling in treatment cohorts:

  • Parent compound: ibogaine plasma levels peak within a few hours and decline to low levels within roughly 24 hours.
  • Metabolite: noribogaine appears rapidly, plateaus, and then clears slowly — remaining quantifiable for days and, at the tissue level, likely for weeks. It distributes extensively and shows high brain uptake in preclinical work.
  • Exposure ratio: integrated over time, noribogaine exposure substantially exceeds ibogaine exposure after a standard oral dose.

In plain terms: for most of the episode a patient is "on" noribogaine, not ibogaine.

Mapping kinetics onto the clinical timeline

Clinical phaseDominant compoundReported phenomena
Hours 1–24Ibogaine (falling), noribogaine (rising)Visionary phase; acute withdrawal suppression
Days 1–7NoribogaineReflective period; low craving; mood elevation
Weeks 2–8Noribogaine (declining)Gradual return of baseline craving pressure in many patients

The correspondence between the metabolite's decline and the return of craving is correlational, not proven causation — but it is the simplest account of why a one-day drug has a one-month effect, and it matches the common clinical advice that the post-treatment weeks are the decisive window.

CYP2D6 and between-person variability

CYP2D6 is among the most polymorphic drug-metabolizing enzymes in humans. Poor metabolizers convert ibogaine slowly (higher, longer parent exposure); ultrarapid metabolizers shift exposure toward the metabolite quickly. The same milligram-per-kilogram dose therefore produces genuinely different pharmacological episodes in different people — one of the strongest scientific arguments for individualized, medically supervised protocols rather than fixed dosing recipes.

The metabolite as a drug candidate

Because noribogaine may carry much of the therapeutic activity with less of the acute phenomenology, it has been pursued as a standalone medication — human phase-1 kinetics are done, and development interest continues. Whether the therapeutic effect survives the removal of the acute experience is an open question with real theoretical stakes, taken up in the analogs entry.
