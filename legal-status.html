Legal status

Ibogaine is banned in some countries, a prescription medicine in others, and simply unregulated in many more. The global picture — and why “unregulated” does not mean “safe”.

Three legal categories

A

Prohibited

Possession and supply are criminal offences.

B

Medically restricted

Classified as a medicine — lawful only through medical channels.

C

Unregulated

Not scheduled; clinics operate with no oversight at all.

Ibogaine is not scheduled under the UN drug conventions — every country decides for itself, which is why the map is a patchwork.

Status we can tie to a primary source

Every row below links to the statute, regulation or regulator's own record it rests on. Scheduling is amended regularly, so check the linked text for the version in force before you rely on it.

CountryCategoryStatus and instrument
United StatesProhibitedSchedule I of the Controlled Substances Act, DEA code 7260 — no accepted medical use in the federal scheme and no approved product (21 CFR 1308.11)
FranceProhibitedAdded to Annex IV of the narcotics list by the order of 12 March 2007, covering Tabernanthe iboga, Tabernanthe manii, ibogaine and their preparations (Légifrance)
United KingdomProhibitedNo named listing; production, supply and import of psychoactive substances are prohibited by the blanket regime of the Psychoactive Substances Act 2016. Possession alone is not an offence except in custodial settings
New ZealandMedically restrictedReclassified as a prescription medicine by the Medicines Classification Committee in November 2009 — the clearest framework anywhere (Medsafe minutes)
AustraliaMedically restrictedListed in Schedule 4 of the Poisons Standard, which makes it prescription-only rather than prohibited (current Poisons Standard)
European UnionMedically restrictedNo authorised medicinal product containing ibogaine exists anywhere in the EU; national scheduling then varies state by state (Esperança 2026 review)

We corrected this page in October 2026. It previously listed Australia as prohibited with no lawful therapeutic products. The Poisons Standard lists ibogaine in Schedule 4, which means prescription-only. The earlier entry was wrong.

The United States, federal and state

US coverage usually stops at "Schedule I", which misses what has changed. Texas has begun funding ibogaine research directly, and it is worth being precise about what that does and does not mean.

LevelWhat is in force
FederalSchedule I, DEA code 7260. Treatment outside an authorised research protocol is unlawful, and prosecutions do happen — a Colorado man was sentenced to 48 months for ibogaine distribution in September 2025 (DEA)
TexasSenate Bill 2308 (2025) created a consortium to run FDA drug-development trials of ibogaine and added Chapter 491 to the Health and Safety Code (enrolled bill). The budget appropriated $50 million to HHSC for fiscal 2026 to implement it (SB 1, Art. IX §18.74)
Texas, where it standsHHSC selected UTHealth Houston as lead institution, but as of October 2026 states that plans submitted so far do not meet Chapter 491's requirements on matching funds and intellectual-property revenue (HHSC)
Other statesWe have found no state that has changed the legal status of ibogaine treatment. Several have considered study or funding bills; none of that makes treatment lawful

State funding is not legalisation. Texas is paying for trials aimed at eventual FDA approval. Ibogaine remains Schedule I federally, and a state appropriation does not create a lawful route to treatment in Texas or anywhere else in the US.

The complete schedules are searchable only through the Thai FDA permit tool and are published in Thai. Secondary sources conflict, and at least one places it in Category 1, the most serious tier. Where it is unregulated

Most of the world's ibogaine treatment happens where the law is simply silent: Mexico (the de facto hub), Costa Rica, and several Caribbean and Central American jurisdictions. No licence is required, there are no inspections and no outcome register — the openness that makes treatment accessible makes quality entirely your problem to verify. Unregulated is not a safety rating. It means nobody is checking.

Three practical consequences: treatment usually means travel · in unregulated countries the vetting burden is entirely yours · and never transport ibogaine across borders — that is a serious offence everywhere prohibition applies, and the sentence above shows it is enforced.
